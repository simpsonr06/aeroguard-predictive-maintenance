## 3. Audit of the Drift-Reference Design & Censoring Asymmetry

### 3.1 The Censoring Asymmetry Problem
In survival and prognostic modeling, the physical distribution of sensor readings depends heavily on the observation window:
* In `train_FD001.txt`, 100% of engine units run until functional breakdown ($RUL = 0$). Consequently, late-life degradation states (elevated temperatures $T_{30}, T_{50}$, depressed pressures $P_{30}, P_{s30}$) are heavily represented.
* In `test_FD001.txt`, engines are censored at arbitrary operational points (mean terminal RUL $\approx 75.8$ cycles). Most engines are stopped while still in healthy cruise or mid-life stages.

**Empirical Consequence:** When clean, uncompromised test telemetry is screened directly against the training baseline, the Two-Sample Kolmogorov-Smirnov test and Population Stability Index (PSI) yield:
* **8 PSI Alert Channels** ($\text{PSI} \ge 0.25$): $s_4, s_7, s_{11}, s_{12}, s_{15}, s_{21}$.
* **6 PSI Warning Channels** ($0.10 \le \text{PSI} < 0.25$): $s_2, s_3, s_8, s_9, s_{13}, s_{14}, s_{17}, s_{20}$.
* **System Status:** `drift_alert` (14 out of 15 informative channels flagged).

Thus, screening operational telemetry against a run-to-failure training reference generates an unmanageable **false-positive alarm rate ($>93\%$ of channels flagged)** under clean conditions.

### 3.2 Dual-Baseline Architecture & Disjoint Fleet Partition
To resolve this without altering model training data or compromising held-out test evaluation:
1. **Model Training & Normalization Reference:** Remains strictly fitted on `train_FD001.txt`, preserving the valid physics of run-to-failure degradation.
2. **Operational Reference Calibration:** Evaluates operational telemetry against an operational reference.
3. **Disjoint Engine-Level Partition:**  
   To eliminate in-sample self-comparison dependencies, the 100 test engines were partitioned into two disjoint subsets:
   * **Calibration Fleet ($\mathcal{E}_{\text{cal}}$, Engines 1–50, 6,564 cycles):** Maintained as an uncorrupted operational reference baseline.
   * **Evaluation Fleet ($\mathcal{E}_{\text{eval}}$, Engines 51–100, 6,532 cycles):** Evaluated both under clean conditions (to measure empirical False Positive Rate) and under attack scenarios (to measure True Positive Rate / Evasion).

### 3.3 Disjoint Drift Detection Outcomes
Evaluating the disjoint engine partition confirms:
* **Clean Evaluation Baseline ($\mathcal{E}_{\text{eval}}$ vs. $\mathcal{E}_{\text{cal}}$):**  
  $\text{PSI Alerts} = 0$, $\text{PSI Warnings} = 0$, $\text{Overall Status} = \mathbf{stable}$.  
  **Empirical False Positive Rate is strictly 0.0%** across completely unseen operational engines.
* **Attack Evasion Dynamics on Disjoint Engines:**
  - `scenario_1_bias_s11_pos_1sigma`: 1 alert channel ($s_{11}$), 0 warnings $\rightarrow$ **Status: `warning` (Evades fleet drift alert)**.
  - `scenario_2_bias_s11_pos_2sigma`: 1 alert channel ($s_{11}$), 0 warnings $\rightarrow$ **Status: `warning` (Evades fleet drift alert)**.
  - `scenario_3_bias_s11_neg_1sigma`: 0 alert channels, 1 warning ($s_{11}$) $\rightarrow$ **Status: `stable` (Completely evades detection)**.
  - `scenario_4_ramp_s11_pos_2sigma`: 1 alert channel ($s_{11}$), 0 warnings $\rightarrow$ **Status: `warning` (Evades fleet drift alert)**.
  - `scenario_5_multisensor_bias`: 3 alert channels ($s_4, s_9, s_{11}$) $\rightarrow$ **Status: `drift_alert` (Detected)**.
  - `scenario_6_frozen_replay`: 3 alert channels, 8 warnings $\rightarrow$ **Status: `drift_alert` (Detected)**.

**Scientific Limitation Statement:** While disjoint engine partitioning resolves in-sample dependency in FD001, an operational reference fleet in commercial aviation requires collecting historical in-service baseline flights from healthy fleet operations under comparable flight conditions. It does not resolve open-world domain shifts across complex multi-condition flight envelopes (e.g., FD002/FD004).

## 4. Sensor Selection & Attack Target Verification (The "Sensor 6" Audit)

The audit investigated why previous documentation reported "14 informative sensors, pruning sensor 6", while Scenario 6 included `sensor_6` in `target_sensors`.

### 4.1 Implementation Code Inspection
* In `src/aircraft_pm/config.py`:
  ```python
  CONSTANT_OR_LOW_VARIANCE_COLS = [
      "setting_3", "sensor_1", "sensor_5", "sensor_10", 
      "sensor_16", "sensor_18", "sensor_19"
  ]
  INFORMATIVE_SENSOR_COLS = [col for col in SENSOR_COLUMNS if col not in CONSTANT_OR_LOW_VARIANCE_COLS]
  ```
  `SENSOR_COLUMNS` contains 21 sensors. Subtracting the 6 pruned sensors yields **exactly 15 active sensors**:  
  $$\mathcal{S}_{\text{active}} = \{s_2, s_3, s_4, s_6, s_7, s_8, s_9, s_{11}, s_{12}, s_{13}, s_{14}, s_{15}, s_{17}, s_{20}, s_{21}\}$$
* In `models/preprocessor.joblib`: Inspection of `feature_names_` confirms that `sensor_6` and its associated temporal features (`sensor_6_roll_mean_5`, `sensor_6_roll_std_5`, `sensor_6_diff_1`, `sensor_6_drift`, etc.) are actively fitted in the 122-feature pipeline.

### 4.2 Finding & Resolution
This was a **documentation transcription error**. While certain historical C-MAPSS literature (e.g., Heimes 2008) prunes 7 sensors including sensor 6 ($P_{15}$, bypass duct pressure) due to low variance in multi-regime datasets, in FD001 sensor 6 exhibits distinct physical variance and was retained in the AeroGuard/AeroSentinel engineering pipeline. The manuscript text, methodology, and figures have been corrected to accurately declare **15 informative sensors**.

## 5. Latency Microbenchmark Reconciliation

### 5.1 Component Sum vs. Composite Pipeline Discrepancy
The initial report noted that component means ($0.11 + 14.93 + 9.47 = 24.51\text{ ms}$) slightly exceeded the reported composite startup latency ($24.37\text{ ms}$).

**Reconciliation:**  
The benchmarks execute in separate, sequential measurement passes ($N=100$ repetitions each). In the standalone hashing and deserialization loops, files are initially accessed cold from disk. By the time the composite pipeline loop executes, OS kernel file-system buffers and page caches are fully warm. The $0.14\text{ ms}$ difference ($<0.6\%$) represents standard OS buffer caching, not a measurement defect.

### 5.2 Single-Engine vs. Fleet Batch Inference
Initial benchmarks reported single-engine inference at $1.62\text{ ms}$ and fleet-wide inference (100 engines) at $1.47\text{ ms}$.

**Reconciliation:**  
1. **Thread-Pool Warm-up Bias:** In initial runs, single-engine inference ran first, absorbing Cython/OpenMP thread-pool initialization overhead.
2. **Fixed Invocation Overhead:** Scikit-learn's `HistGradientBoostingRegressor.predict()` incurs a fixed Python-to-C wrapper overhead of $\approx 1.1\text{ ms}$ for array shape validation and memory layout checks (`check_array`).
3. **Interleaved Profiling Results ($N=100$, Interleaved Execution):**
   - Single-Engine Inference ($1 \times 122$ matrix): $\text{Mean} = \mathbf{1.39\text{ ms}}$, $\text{Median} = \mathbf{1.16\text{ ms}}$.
   - Fleet-Wide Batch Inference ($100 \times 122$ matrix): $\text{Mean} = \mathbf{1.34\text{ ms}}$, $\text{Median} = \mathbf{1.17\text{ ms}}$ (amortized to **$13.4\ \mu\text{s}$ per engine**).

Evaluating 100 engines in a single contiguous NumPy matrix incurs virtually the same base dispatch overhead as a single row, proving that batch inference achieves high operational efficiency.
